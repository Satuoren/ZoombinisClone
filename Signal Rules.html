      Not measured by this game, and why
      • CT: Algorithmic thinking. Every player writes a rule on every run, so there is nothing that varies between players.
      • CT: Decomposition. 97–100% of 3-star rules use two conditions, so a two-condition rule is what doing well requires, not a sign of breaking the problem down. The levels also split the problem up for the player.
      • CT: Abstraction. Only 1–3 sensors are offered, and the levels force which ones work: no clock-only rule clears Days 1–3.
      • CT: Transfer. The rule carries over to the next day automatically, and 94% of rules that clear Day 4 also clear Day 5. Reusing a rule is the default, not evidence. Not carrying the rule into Day 5 would make this measurable.
      • CT: Tracing. Pausing and stepping show the player used the tool, not whether they understood what they saw. Pauses and steps are still logged.

      Does it really measure CT and systems thinking? Open questions

      • Every link above is a behavioral proxy. None is shown to be CT or systems thinking until it is checked against something outside the game.
      • The debrief names the main problem after each run, so stocks-and-flows and interconnection behavior partly reflects following a hint. Comparing players who see that line with players who don’t would separate the two.
      • The chance rates (29% for a one-part fix on Day 3, 68% gridlock on Festival Day) come from testing every possible rule in the simulation. Real players do not pick rules uniformly, so treat them as a reference point, not an exact baseline. Recompute them if a level is retuned.
      • Content check: ask two or three researchers to map each skill link to a CT or systems-thinking definition and mark the ones that do not fit.
      • Convergent check: compare in-game behavior with a validated CT test, Bebras-style items, or a stock-and-flow task (for example, a bathtub-dynamics item) from the same players.
      • Confounds to rule out: prior traffic-game experience, reading load in the rule builder, and players who change the rule at random until the debrief turns green.
